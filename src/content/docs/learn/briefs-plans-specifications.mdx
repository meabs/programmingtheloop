title: "2. Briefs, plans and specifications"
description: Chapter 2 — distinguish task requests, plans and executable criteria.
---

Chapter 2 distinguishes three documents that are often blurred together: the brief says what outcome is wanted; the plan describes an approach; the specification makes behavior testable.

## Make the outcome observable

The book’s inventory example makes outcomes concrete: known stock returns an available quantity, fully reserved stock returns unavailable, an unknown SKU returns not found, and an unreachable or out-of-contract service produces a logged error response. It also distinguishes a contract freshness check from demonstrating real behavior against a running service.

The brief can be the problem. Ambiguous or conflicting criteria leave the agent to choose what success means. A plan may improve sequencing, but only a check tied to the required behavior can provide evidence about the result.

## Try this

Write acceptance criteria as observable cases. Mark each as locally testable, dependent on an external service, or requiring human judgement. Ensure the final report distinguishes passed checks from criteria that were not verified.

## Chapter checklist

- Give every non-trivial task a brief with acceptance criteria and an out-of-scope line.
- Ask the agent to restate the goal and identify contradictions or gaps before it starts.
- Delete or archive temporary plans when work merges.
- Keep durable behavior in tests; give long-lived requirements an owner.
- Keep data-model and boundary decisions with a human.
