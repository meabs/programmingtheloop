title: "10. Safety, cost and the human in the loop"
description: Chapter 10 — account for operational risk, expense and human oversight.
---

Chapter 10 draws a practical line around security, cost and human oversight. Agent capability does not remove the need to control permissions, protect work and evaluate the consequences of mistakes.

The book describes practical opinion about tools that change quickly and explicitly says its experiments are small. Its safety and cost advice should be read as engineering guidance with local validation, not as legal, security or financial advice.

One safety frame in Chapter 10 is the “lethal trifecta”: an agent should not combine access to private data, untrusted content and external communication. If permission prompts are skipped, the author recommends a sandbox without production credentials. For cost, the book recommends spending effort where its evidence suggests waste occurs (blind search and heavy always-loaded context) and measuring value as well as tokens.

## Try this

Set the least access needed for a task, define which actions require review, keep backups, and decide what costs and failure modes you will monitor. Revisit those choices when the tools or work change.

The chapter also recommends preserving your own key skills and batching check-ins. Token counts should be compared within one agent tool; tokens are not the same as value.
