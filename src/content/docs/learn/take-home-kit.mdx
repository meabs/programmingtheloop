The final chapter turns the book into an adoption kit. Its Default, Conditional and Experimental labels describe how to adopt a practice, not the strength of evidence for a claim.

Use the recommendations as starting points. Choose practices that solve an observed problem, preserve the information needed to check them, and keep historical experiment results stable when later evidence changes a view.

## A starting checklist

- State the task outcome and observable acceptance criteria.
- Supply the authoritative facts the agent cannot discover from its repository.
- Break work into small changes with explicit stopping conditions.
- Check both the implementation and whether the checks cover the required behavior.
- Record friction and measured results with enough context to reproduce them.
- Match human review and permissions to the risk of the change.

## Three rules for Monday morning

The author’s summary is: put recurring fixes in scripts rather than prose; keep local context lean and document the boundaries the agent cannot see; never trust a green report without an independent check. The book’s examples and rationale are in the linked chapters above.

## Adopt in stages

The book distinguishes **Default** practices (cheap, reversible and broadly useful), **Conditional** practices (adopt after a targeted trial on your work), and **Experimental** practices (pilot separately with an exit test). These describe adoption choices, not statistical evidence strength. The opening baseline is a reliable build, test and lint command, a short `AGENTS.md`, and briefs with acceptance criteria; move to added layers only when observed friction justifies them.
